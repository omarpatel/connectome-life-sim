A real brain

The wiring is real. All of it.

In 2026 the Janelia FlyEM team published the complete connectome of an adult male fruit fly: brain, optic lobes and nerve cord, from electron microscopy. Every fly in this cage runs that dataset verbatim: — neurons and — connections of five or more synapses. Nothing about a fly's behaviour is scripted; when one walks to the food, it is the wiring doing it.

A living cage

It runs whether or not anyone is watching.

One world, ten ticks a second, day and night. Flies find food by smell, walk on the glass, take off under their own wing motor neurons, breed when they can, and die when they run out of energy. The world is deterministic: from a snapshot, anyone can replay it and check that the hash the operator posted to Solana is the truth.

1How it works

A fly's lifecycle and the local ledger.

The local Solana program tracks creature ownership and accounting. These are simulation mechanics using test SOL, not a token offering or a production deployment. The technical account describes the ledger's rules and custody assumptions.

  1. Born. Two flies with enough energy and the neurosecretory gate open produce a child: its brain is a crossover of its parents' connection weights with a few mutations. The newborn is minted and put on offer at a price that rises with its generation, holds for — minutes, then falls over — minutes to —% of it, where it stays until someone buys.
  2. Bought. You pay the offer's price at that moment, never more than the one you saw. 60% goes into the fly's own vault, 15% into the cage's metabolism (which sets how many flies the cage can hold), 15% into the pool, and 10% to its parent's vault.
  3. Lives. Every — epochs (— minutes) a quarter of the simulated pool is allocated among living flies by points: survival, foraging, energy, age, maturity, births and generation.
  4. Dies. Starved, exhausted in flight, drowned, old age. The NFT burns. The vault is paid out: 40% split among its living children, 10% credited to you, 35% back to metabolism, 15% to the pool. You can withdraw your credit at any time.

2Local accounts

Use the local world.

  1. Open the cage. Observe without signing in, or create a local Instar account to exercise the ledger. The world holds that account's keypair; alternatively, connect a wallet configured for your local cluster.
  2. Use test SOL only. The Account window provides an airdrop control on supported test clusters. Never send real funds to this development world.
  3. Buy a newborn from the Market window. It is minted to your wallet as a Core NFT in the Instar collection; holding the asset is what makes you its keeper. From the Mine window you can list it for resale, transfer it, cull it, and withdraw what you are owed; with an Instar account you can also name its lineage.

3Check it yourself

Don't take the operator's word.

Every — ticks the engine hashes the whole world: every fly, every position, every neuron it touched. The operator posts that 64-bit hash to the World account on Solana. Anyone can take the world's snapshot, replay the same ticks on their own machine with the same engine, hash it, and compare with what is on chain. Same bytes, honest world; different bytes, caught.

  1. Use this repository and install Node 22 or newer.
  2. INSTAR_URL=this site npm run verify:epoch — it fetches the snapshot, replays one epoch, reads the World account, and prints VERIFIED or MISMATCH.
  3. On a desktop with 4 GB free, the cage page can run the same replay in your browser.

4Questions
Is this AI?

No. There is no neural network in the machine-learning sense and no model was trained. Each fly is the published wiring of a real fly's nervous system, run as an integer threshold model. The paper says exactly what is real and what is not.

Is it really a fly brain?

It is really the fly's connectome: which neuron connects to which, and how strongly, for all — neurons. What the dataset does not give (how each neuron fires, muscles, a body) the engine supplies with one simple rule for every cell. So: driven by the real wiring, not a simulation of biology. We never say otherwise.

What happens when my fly dies?

Its NFT is burned and its vault is paid out by rule: 40% to its living children, 10% credited to you, the rest back into the cage. Your credit is yours to withdraw whenever you like. With no living children the 40% goes to the cage instead. Death is common.

Should I use real funds?

No. This is a local development world. Use test SOL and disposable development accounts only.

Who holds my keys?

Your choice. With an Instar account the operator holds a keypair for you, sealed under the world's master key; your PIN only signs you in, and the operator can spend from it. That is a trust assumption. With your own wallet, nobody but you: the page builds transactions from the program's IDL and your wallet signs them.

What if the operator disappears?

The program has a way out that needs nobody's permission. After 90 days without an operator action anyone can begin wind-down; keepers reclaim their vaults; 180 days after wind-down began, whatever was not claimed goes to the recovery address, which the operator can change until wind-down begins and never after. The program's own rent can be reclaimed too. No lamport is stranded by design.

How many flies can the cage hold?

Two limits, the smaller wins: the economy's (8 plus 20 per SOL of metabolism, up to 40) and the server's compute budget at 80 ms per tick. Running — neurons per fly is expensive; the world measures the cost every epoch and reports the capacity live. The cage refills: when the population has sat below half its capacity for a minute, new founders are born to bring it back, so a bigger metabolism is a fuller cage.

Can the operator move ledger funds?

The operator can move metabolism and pool balances with the public withdraw_treasury instruction. Creature vaults and keeper credits follow separate program rules. These mechanics are for local test funds only.

Why did my fly die minutes after I bought it?

Flight costs 20–28× what standing does and many flies take off with too little energy to land; founders start hungry. Watch a fly for a while before buying it: its energy, its mode and what it fired are in the inspector.

Why Solana?

The local program records creature lifecycle operations and state commitments. Metaplex Core represents owned creatures as assets.

What is included?

The repository includes the Rust/WebAssembly engine, Anchor program, world process, site, census converter and verifier. The connectome is Janelia's, CC BY 4.0.
